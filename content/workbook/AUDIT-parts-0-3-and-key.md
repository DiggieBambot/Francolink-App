### A1 · Exercice 3, question 3 — question and answer don't match
**This is the one real key defect.** The question asks for the sound of the
underlined part of four words:

> Read aloud and write the sound of the underlined part: bonj**OU**r · tr**OI**s · ch**AT** · s**OEU**r.

The key answers `ou = « oo » · oi = « wa » · ch = « sh » · œu = « euh »`.

Item 3 answers **ch**, but the question underlines **AT**. The learner is asked
about one thing and marked on another.

**Fix — change the question** so the key becomes correct, since *ch* is a
letter-combination worth teaching and *at* is not:

> 3. Read aloud and write the sound of the underlined part: bonj**OU**r · tr**OI**s · **CH**at · s**OEU**r.

### A2 · Exercice 4, question 4 — correct answer, muddled reasoning
The verdict (*False*) is right, but the explanation opens with an irrelevant
clause about *parlez* being "followed by nothing here", which is not why there
is no liaison.

**Replace the key entry with:**

> 4. **False.** A liaison needs the *next* word to begin with a vowel sound.
> In « vous parlez », the second word begins with the consonant **p**, so the
> **s** of *vous* stays silent. Compare « vous **a**vez » → « vou-**z**avez ».

### A3 · Exercice 13, question 4 — malformed prompt
> 4. Ask for the missing word: « Je vais ______ ? » (where)

You cannot fill that blank with *où* — the answer the key gives is *Où vas-tu ?*,
a different sentence. **Replace the question with:**

> 4. Turn into a question with **où**: « Tu vas à Paris. » → ______
> *(Key: Où vas-tu ?)*

### A4 · Exercice 28, question 3 — contains its own answer
> 3. Prediction: « Il (pleuvoir → **pleuvra**) ______ demain. »

**Delete the gloss:** « Il (pleuvoir) ______ demain. »

### B2 · §1.4 — the CaReFuL mnemonic is stated inside-out
> "A classic memory key groups the usually-silent final consonants; a common
> mnemonic is to remember they are NOT in the English word CaReFuL (c, r, f, l
> are the finals you often DO pronounce)."

The reader has to hold a double negative across a semicolon to extract a simple
rule. **Replace with:**

> **The CaReFuL rule.** Most final consonants are silent. The usual exceptions
> are **c, r, f, l** — the consonants in the English word **CaReFuL**. So the
> final letters of *avec, bonjour, chef* and *animal* are pronounced, while the
> ones in *temps, chat, grand* and *nez* are not.

### B3 · §1.1 — the alphabet table mixes letter-names with sound-values
The column is headed *Nom (son)*, and most rows work: **H** *muet (silent)* →
*heureux* demonstrates the silence. But three rows give a letter-name whose
sound the example word does not make:

| Row | Says | Example | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| G | « jé » | garçon | *garçon* has the hard /g/, not the /ʒ/ of the name |
| X | « iks » | exact | in *exact*, x is /gz/ — neither /iks/ nor /ks/ |
| C | « sé » | ciel | works, but only by luck — *car* would not |

A beginner reading this as a pronunciation guide will draw the wrong
conclusion. Two options: relabel the column **Nom de la lettre** and add a
sentence saying the example is only a word beginning with that letter; or swap
the examples for ones that demonstrate the named sound. **Recommend the first**
— it is one line, and §1.3 already teaches the letter combinations properly.

Whichever is chosen, add a note to the **X** row: *x* is /ks/ in *taxi* but
/gz/ in *exact*, and silent in *deux*.

### B4 · §2.5 — the "ball rule" contradicts its own table
The rule is given as: no ball → *faire de*, ball → *jouer à*. The table then
lists **jouer aux cartes** and **jouer aux échecs** under the ball column. The
header hedges with "with ball / game", but the memory hook the learner was just
given is broken by the second and third examples they read.

**Restate the rule to match the table:**

> 🎾 **The rule.** A sport you *do* → **faire du / de la** (faire du vélo, de la
> natation). A sport or game you *play against someone* → **jouer au / aux**
> (jouer au football, aux cartes, aux échecs). Instruments are the odd ones
> out: **jouer du / de la** (jouer du piano).

### C1 · Exercice 32.5 — the same defect I flagged in the book's Exercice 13.4
> 5. Négatif : (tu / oublier) « Ne ______ pas ! » *(key: n'oublie)*

Filling the blank yields « **Ne n'oublie** pas ! » — the negative particle
twice. Exactly the fault criticised in A3 above, reproduced in my own draft.

**Fixed:** « ______ pas ton passeport ! » · key **N'oublie**.

### C2 · Exercice 37.1 and C3 · Exercice 45.3 — « déjà » in the wrong place
> Quand nous sommes arrivés, le film (commencer) ______ déjà.

The adverb belongs *inside* the compound tense — *le film avait **déjà**
commencé* — but the blank sits before it, so the correct answer produces the
unnatural « avait commencé déjà ».

**Fixed** in both places by splitting the blank around the adverb, which also
teaches the placement rather than hiding it:

> le film ______ déjà ______ . *(commencer)* · key: **avait / commencé**
> il ______ déjà ______ . *(partir)* · key: **était / parti**

### C4 · Exercice 41.2 — key doesn't fit the sentence
> 2. Le film est long ; ______, il est excellent. *(key: certes… mais)*

*Certes … mais* is a two-part frame and cannot go in one blank mid-sentence.
**Fixed:** « ______, le film est long, ______ il est excellent. » ·
key **Certes … mais**.

### C5 · Exercice 42 — mislabelled item
The heading says *Formez le gérondif*, but item 4 (*être → Étant malade, il est
resté chez lui*) has no **en** and is a participe présent, not a gérondif.

**Fixed:** retitled *Gérondif ou participe présent ?* with a line explaining
that items 1–3 take **en** and item 4 does not.
