The TCF speaking format for TCF Canada expression orale is 12 minutes face to face with an examiner, in three tasks: a 2-minute guided interview, a 5 minute 30 role play in which you ask the questions, and a 4 minute 30 monologue giving your point of view. Only the role play has preparation time, 2 minutes of it. The test is marked out of 20, NCLC 7 starts at 10, and the [TCF score chart](/guides/tcf-canada/score-chart) has every level.

Twelve minutes is short. There is no time to warm up, so the first minute counts as much as the last.

## What are the three TCF Canada speaking tasks?

The three tasks move from talking about yourself, to getting information from someone, to defending an opinion. This is how [France Éducation International](https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada?langue=fr) sets them out:

| Task | What happens | Time | Preparation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tâche 1: entretien dirigé | You talk with someone you do not know, the examiner | 2 minutes | None |
| Tâche 2: exercice en interaction | You get information in an everyday situation, with roles given in the instructions | 5 minutes 30 | 2 minutes, included in the 5 minutes 30 |
| Tâche 3: expression d'un point de vue | You speak spontaneously, continuously and persuasively on a question the examiner chooses | 4 minutes 30 | None |

## How is the speaking test marked?

It is marked out of 20 and converted by IRCC to an NCLC level. According to France Éducation International, you are assessed on your ability to:

- talk about yourself and your family and working life;
- ask questions suited to the situation;
- give your opinion, explain the advantages and disadvantages of a plan, agree and disagree;
- present a clear, structured argument in a style that fits the context;
- present complex subjects in detail and in order, develop them and conclude.

Each item on that list maps to one of the tasks. The first is Tâche 1, the second is Tâche 2, and the last three are Tâche 3.

## What TCF Canada speaking score do you need?

You need 10 out of 20 for NCLC 7. For citizenship the requirement is lower: CLB 4 in speaking and listening, which is 4 out of 20 in speaking.

| Mark out of 20 | NCLC |
| --- | --- |
| 4–5 | 4 |
| 6 | 5 |
| 7–9 | 6 |
| 10–11 | 7 |
| 12–13 | 8 |
| 14–15 | 9 |
| 16–20 | 10 and above |

Source: IRCC, [TCF Canada equivalency chart](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-requirements/language-testing.html), and IRCC's [citizenship language requirement](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/become-canadian-citizen/eligibility/language-proof.html) for applicants aged 18 to 54.

## How do you handle Tâche 1, the guided interview?

Have a two-minute account of yourself ready, and deliver it as a conversation, not a recitation. The examiner is checking that you can exchange with a stranger, so answer the question asked, then add one detail they did not ask for. That keeps the exchange going without you having to wait for the next question.

The topics are predictable: who you are, where you are from, your work or studies, your family, what you do in your free time, and why you want to live in Canada. Prepare vocabulary for each. Do not memorise a script word for word. A learnt text sounds learnt, and it falls apart the moment the examiner asks something slightly different.

Use the past and the future as well as the present. *J'ai travaillé cinq ans comme infirmière. Au Canada, j'aimerais…* Three tenses in the first two minutes shows range early.

## How do you handle Tâche 2, the role play?

Ask a lot of varied questions, and react to the answers. In Tâche 2 you are the one who needs information, and the instructions tell you who you are and who the examiner is playing. A candidate who asks three questions and waits has not done the task.

Use the 2 minutes of preparation to note **topics**, not sentences. Say the scenario is:

> Vous voulez vous inscrire dans une salle de sport près de chez vous. Vous posez des questions à l'employé.

Your notes might be one word per line: *horaires, prix, engagement, cours collectifs, coach, parking, essai gratuit, annulation*. That is eight questions' worth of material, and you will not run dry at minute three.

Then vary the question forms, because that variety is part of what is being assessed:

- *Est-ce que la salle est ouverte le dimanche ?*
- *Quels sont vos horaires en semaine ?*
- *Combien coûte l'abonnement mensuel ?*
- *Y a-t-il des cours collectifs le soir ?*
- *Si je veux arrêter, je dois prévenir combien de temps à l'avance ?*

Keep the register polite and formal: you are speaking to an employee you do not know, so *vous* throughout. Reacting to the answers, with *ah d'accord*, *et dans ce cas…*, or a follow-up question, is what turns a list of questions into an interaction.

## How do you handle Tâche 3, the point of view?

Take a clear position in your first sentence, support it with two or three arguments that each have an example, acknowledge the other side, and conclude. You have no preparation, and 4 minutes 30 is a long time to talk alone. A structure is what stops you running out.

Say the examiner asks:

> Pensez-vous que le télétravail est une bonne chose pour les employés ?

A shape you can use for any question:

1. **Position.** *À mon avis, le télétravail est plutôt positif, mais pas pour tout le monde.*
2. **First argument and example.** *D'abord, on gagne du temps. Quand je travaillais à Lagos, je passais deux heures par jour dans les transports.*
3. **Second argument and example.** *Ensuite…*
4. **The other side.** *Cela dit, il faut reconnaître que certains se sentent isolés…*
5. **Conclusion.** *En somme, je pense que la meilleure solution est un mélange des deux.*

The examiner may push back or ask you to go further. That is not a sign you are doing badly. It is the task: defending your view when challenged.

## What is the most common mistake in the speaking test?

Stopping too early. In Tâche 3 especially, a candidate who says their opinion in thirty seconds and falls silent has not shown the "développer et conclure" France Éducation International asks for, however good the French in those thirty seconds was. Practise until four and a half minutes of structured talk feels normal.

The second is freezing on a missing word. Talk around it. *Le truc qu'on utilise pour…* keeps you speaking and is itself a skill the examiner hears.

## How should you practise for TCF Canada expression orale?

Speak to a person, under the real timings, and get told what went wrong. Speaking cannot be practised properly alone: you need someone to play the examiner in Tâche 2 and to push back in Tâche 3. Pronunciation, fluency and register are also the hardest things to judge in yourself.

Since 1 September 2026, France Éducation International is not accepting re-marking requests for the speaking and writing parts, with the service expected back in autumn 2027. Walking in prepared is the only lever you have.
